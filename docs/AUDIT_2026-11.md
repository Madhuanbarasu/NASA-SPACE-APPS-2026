# Audit and fixes, 2026-11-07

Scope: disk accuracy, terrain safeguards, DEM and error-map associations, validation integrity, V-14 testing, USGS/PGDA documentation. Architecture, API and interface are unchanged; `frontend/` and `start.py` were not modified. Algorithm version 1.1.1 -> 1.2.0 (cached results from 1.1.1 are not reused).

Baseline before any change (sandbox test kernels): 38 passed, 1 skipped, 109.6 s. After: 50 passed, 1 skipped, 141.7 s. Evidence: `docs/validation_evidence/`.

## Defects found and fixed

**Horizon uncertainty modelling & near-field artifact resolution (`engine/horizon.py`, `engine/events.py`).**
The initial error-map implementation computed per-bin uncertainty as `sigma_H = atan(sigma_block / d)`. For the finest 5m DEM at default observer height (obs_h_m = 0.0), rays finding their peak at the first half-pixel step (d = 2.5 m) divided by 2.5 m, yielding an artificial 7.3888 deg spike across 1,591 of 3,600 bins (44% of the horizon). This artifact caused `_flag_intervals` to falsely mark clear solar tracking windows (solar elevation ~1.0-1.5 deg < 7.3888 deg) as `uncertain: True`.
Resolution: Introduced geodetic error spatial correlation for blocker points sampled from the same DEM product as the observer: `rho(d) = exp(-0.5 * (d / L_corr)^2)` with LOLA correlation length `L_corr = 250.0 m`. Relative vertical uncertainty `sigma_dz = sqrt((sigma_block^2 + sigma_site^2) * (1 - rho(d)))` smoothly approaches 0 as d -> 0, bounding angular uncertainty by local slope roughness (~0.06 deg at d = 2.5 m instead of 7.3888 deg). Blocker points from coarser/different products remain uncorrelated (`rho = 0`). Spurious uncertainty flags on clear solar tracking windows are completely eliminated.

**Test portability & registry isolation (`engine/dem.py`, `acquire.py`, `tests/test_pgda90_integration.py`).**
Hardcoded absolute machine-specific paths (e.g. `C:\Users\THILAKHAESH\...`) in `data/dem/registry.json` and `acquire.py` broke test portability on other environments. Furthermore, `test_pgda90_integration.py`'s `setup_real_dem` fixture overwrote `settings.DEM_DIR / "registry.json"` without restoring synthetic test entries, breaking subsequent test suites (`test_audit_fixes.py`, `test_engine.py`) when executed in arbitrary order.
Resolution: Replaced all absolute paths in `data/dem/registry.json` with portable relative paths (e.g. `pgda78_site01/...`, `pgda90_80m/...`). Added `resolve_dem_path` in `engine/dem.py` with multi-tier path resolution (active `DEM_DIR` -> repo `data/dem` fallback). Updated `acquire.py` (`_portable_dem_path`, `cmd_verify`) to handle relative paths. Refactored `setup_real_dem` fixture to cleanly save and restore registry state upon teardown, ensuring full test independence regardless of execution order (`pytest` forward and reverse suites).

**5m-to-80m transition, valid footprint & overlap offsets (`settings.py`, `docs/TRACEABILITY.md`).**
`Site01_final_adj_5mpp_surf.tif` has 100% valid data (10,240,000 pixels, 0 NaNs) across its entire 16 km x 16 km footprint (8,000 m radius from Site01 center at (-11000, -12000)). However, `settings.DEM_WINDOW_HALF_M["5m"]` was hardcoded to 4,000.0 m, prematurely cutting off 5m ray marching at 4 km and forcing handoff to 80m regional DEM between 4 km and 8 km.
Resolution: Expanded `settings.DEM_WINDOW_HALF_M["5m"]` to 8,000.0 m, matching the actual valid footprint radius and allowing the 5m product to serve rays out to 7,990 m before falling through to 80m. Measured empirical elevation differences across 23,572 overlap grid points in the 4-8 km ring: mean offset (5m - 80m) = +1.0726 m, std = 6.2428 m, RMS = 6.3343 m, median = +1.5292 m, 5th-95th percentile [-10.1862, +10.7050] m. *(Method note added in Phase 2: these figures sample the 80 m DEM at the nearest pixel (rasterio `sample`) on the 4-8 km annulus. They are not the cross-product discrepancy allowance, which is defined with bilinear interpolation over the window intersection and measures mean -0.053 m, std 1.223 m, RMS 1.224 m for the same products; see "Cross-product statistics: method and region" below.)* Documented intermediate coverage options (PGDA 90 publishes regional 20m `LDEM_80S_20MPP_ADJ.TIF` at 2.6 GB; no 40m product published).

**Disk accuracy (`engine/visibility.py`).** Disks below 60 deg were solved in a flattened plane, x = dAz cos El_T, y = dEl, whose error grows as rho^2 tan(el)/2. Measured against an independent exact spherical integration: up to 1.3e-2 deg in Earth edge clearance and 3.3e-3 in visible fraction near 60 deg, and about 1e-3 deg for Earth at polar elevations (7-12 deg), which at Earth's slow libration motion can move DTE event times by tens of seconds. Replaced by a closed-form spherical-cap solution against the same piecewise-linear horizon (cap integrals verified to 7e-12; agreement with the reference 5.7e-9 in fraction, 4.2e-10 deg in clearance). The combined Sun-Earth clearance now includes the exact solar limb point farthest from Earth, so pure-occultation boundaries are exact. The quick-test azimuth half-width now uses the exact cap formula. The README's "step of order 1e-4 deg" at the 60 deg switch was wrong; measured: 7.1e-4 deg.

**Terrain safeguards (`engine/dem.py`).** A registered DEM whose file was missing was skipped silently while the horizon cache key (built from every registered product) stayed the same, so a stale or substituted horizon could be served; toggling synthetic terrain also left keys unchanged. Now: missing or resized files are refused with the reason wherever they would have been used; cache keys, result IDs and tiles use only the products usable now, with their error maps; heights beyond +/-20 km are nodata; registration refuses undeclared nodata; `acquire verify` re-hashes everything; `/api/status` and `acquire status` list problems.

**DEM / error-map association (`acquire.py`, `engine/horizon.py`).** Registering any real error map failed: the "values below 20 may be km" check rejected uncertainties of 0.3-5 m. Sigma was read from the finest product's error map that had data at a point, not from the product that supplied the blocking sample (wrong whenever a finer product extends past its distance band). Error maps survived replacement of their DEM and were not in cache keys. Now each error map is checked against its DEM (projection, pixel size, pixel grid, overlap), recorded with the DEM's SHA-256, dropped when that DEM is replaced or re-registered with other bytes, included in cache keys, and each bin reads only the error map of the product the horizon kernel reports for its blocking sample.

**Validation integrity (`validation/common.py`, `run_validation.py`, `engine/provenance.py`, `fingerprint.py`).** An external result file could report any test ID (including V-02e) and could turn a failed automated V-14 into a pass; status words were not checked; tests without a blueprint ID (label integrity, merge guard) could fail without blocking a Validated label; results were labelled synthetic if any usable product was synthetic, not from the terrain they used. Now: each script may report only its own IDs, a failure on either side wins, status words are checked, the report and external files carry a test-suite fingerprint, a Validated label needs pytest exit code 0 and a matching suite fingerprint, and labels follow the result's own terrain.

**V-14 (`validation/v14_month.py`, `lunaops/testing.py`, pytest form).** The month script brute-forced in daily chunks and dropped every window that crossed a chunk boundary, so windows longer than a day (the typical polar case) were never tested; events were matched to the nearest event of either direction; false events were not checked; a 1-day 1-site run could report "passed". Now the series is continuous across chunks, windows open at either end are handled, matching is direction-aware, unmatched detector events must be confirmed by dense evaluation, and short runs report "not run". Full form result: 955 required edges, 0 missed, 0 beyond 1.0 s, 0 spurious, worst 0.5 s. The previously published "423 windows" are superseded.

**Documentation (README, ATTRIBUTION, `manifests/datasets.json`, checked against the PGDA 69/78/81/90 pages and the ScienceBase item on 2026-11-07).** Added exact file names, sizes and direct links: `ldem_87s_5mpp.tif` (81, no error map), `<Site>_final_adj_5mpp_surf.tif` / `_toterr.tif` (78), `LDEM_*_ADJ.TIF` / `LDEM_*_ADJ_ERR.TIF` (90; the 10 m error map is `LDEM_83S_10MPP_ADJ_ERR.TIF`, 7.5 GB). PGDA 78 ROIs are per-site `.tgz` archives, not one zip; `.tgz/.tar.gz/.tar` support added with path-traversal checks, and IDs include the archive name. The USGS release (McClernan 2024, doi:10.5066/P1MEQ6UK) contains LGRS ACC navigation grids at 1 km/100 m/10 m for nine regions, not region outlines; entries are now labelled as grid bounding boxes, and bounding boxes are read from the shapefile header (O(1) for 10 m grids). PGDA 69 Earth-visibility maps use an any-part rule, so V-10 must use the "any" rule.

## Phase 2: horizon uncertainty corrections (algorithm 1.3.0; horizon values unchanged)

Scope: how horizon **uncertainty** is computed, flagged and reported. The ray marcher, its sampling, stopping rule and product order are untouched. `tests/test_phase2_golden.py` compares horizon elevations, incompleteness, blocking distances and ground heights (4 sites, 2 observer heights) and a 30-day analysis with 13 events against values recorded from unmodified `main` (7ebe3a4) before any edit. All are identical; only flags and provenance may differ. No NASA data, error maps, registry entries or kernels were changed.

**Defects fixed.**
- *Site product found by level.* `site_wi` and `site_pix` took the first window with the site's level. With two products of one resolution (PGDA 78 site plus PGDA 81 mosaic) the wrong error map and correlation branch could be used. Now `dem.site_height_window` returns the window index, and the horizon kernel's per-bin window index is the only product identity used for the error map, the same/cross-product decision and the allowance pair.
- *Sigma formula.* The old formula was `atan(sqrt((s_b^2 + s_s^2)(1 - rho))/d)` within a product and `atan(s_b/d)` across products. The first is only valid when s_b = s_s (as rho → 1 it gave 0 instead of (s_b − s_s)^2). The second dropped the site's own error, and both used the arc distance without the geometric factor. It is replaced by exact first-order propagation:
  - with v = P_b − P_s and sin e = v·û/|v|: g_b = (p̂·û − sin e v̂·p̂)/(|v| cos e) and g_s = −cos e/|v|;
  - Var e = g_b² s_b² + g_s² s_s² + 2 g_b g_s ρ s_b s_s + g_b² s_x²;
  - it is evaluated as (g_b s_b + ρ g_s s_s)² + (1 − ρ²) g_s² s_s² + g_b² s_x², a sum of non-negative squares. This removes a cancellation residue the expanded form left at ρ = 1: 9e-10 rad instead of 0.
  - Within a product, ρ(d) = exp(−d²/2L²) is a positive-definite kernel. Across products ρ = 0, which is conservative.
- *L_corr labelled.* **L_corr = 250 m is an uncalibrated assumption (no published source); horizon uncertainty and uncertain flags depend on it.** That exact text appears in:
  - horizon parameters;
  - result parameters and warnings;
  - the horizon, events and power-indicator label reasons, which keep those quantities "Pending validation";
  - every CSV preamble.
- *Cross-product discrepancy allowance.* s_x = RMS(fine − coarse) is measured in the overlap of the two windows around the site. The stride is the coarse/fine pixel ratio, with at most 40 000 points and at least 100 pairs. It is a **conservative allowance (includes measured bias); not a calibrated random 1-sigma**. It is reported with n, mean, std and RMS.
  - With fewer than 100 pairs the discrepancy is **unknown, never zero**: those bins get `sigma_deg` null, keep a labelled `sigma_lower_bound_deg`, set `discrepancy_unknown`, force "uncertain" on every interval that crosses them, and add a result warning.
- *Linear range.* Bins with sigma above 5° set `sigma_nonlinear`, and intervals crossing them are uncertain whatever their clearance.
- *Flags.*
  - The threshold is k·(largest sigma over the bins the disk spans): the limiting edge, not the centre sigma.
  - A window is uncertain if max_t (g − kσ) < 0, the same rule as before.
  - Gaps are now flagged: uncertain if min_t (g + kσ) > 0, i.e. no sample is hidden by more than kσ.
  - Every interval is also evaluated at its midpoint, so short intervals are no longer `None`.
  - Intervals list `uncertain_reasons` (`below_k_sigma`, `sigma_nonlinear`, `cross_product_discrepancy_unknown`) and `near_field`.
  - Overlap windows inherit both flags from their parents (OR; `None` only if both parents are `None`).
  - Summaries add `uncertain_gaps` and `near_field_windows`. k = 1, unchanged.
- *Near field.* Bins whose blocker lies within 2 site-product pixels are flagged `near_field`, as is any interval whose target crosses them. There is a result warning, and the observer default stays 0 m.
- *Cache keys.* `horizon_key` covers L_corr, the correlation model, `SIGMA_NONLINEAR_DEG`, the near-field pixel count, k and every `CROSS_PRODUCT_SAMPLING` entry. `result_id` covers k.
- *Fallback folders.* `resolve_dem_path` no longer falls back silently to the repository's `data/dem`. It searches `DEM_DIR` and then only the folders listed in `LUNAOPS_DEM_FALLBACK_DIRS`, and each horizon level records where its file was found.

**Corrections to the approved plan, found during implementation.**
1. *Gap rule.* The plan stated "max_t (g + kσ) > 0". Near every gap edge g → 0, so that rule would flag every gap adjacent to a window. The implemented rule is the mirror of the window rule: min_t (g + kσ) > 0. It is tested in `test_gap_rule_uses_the_deepest_sample`.
2. *Nonlinear example.* For 5 m at 20 m the plan quoted about 19.5°; first-order propagation gives about 14.3°. The test checks the threshold, not the figure.

### Phase 2 correction: variance regularization at the correlation length (after real-data testing)

**Finding.** Run locally on the real PGDA 78 Site01 / PGDA 90 80 m files, `test_pgda90_integration.py` gave 11 passed and 1 failed. `test_near_field_uncertainty_correlation_eliminates_7_deg_artifact` reported a near-field (2.5 m) sigma of 2.842620025° against its 0.15° threshold.

**Cause: an implementation defect, not an invalid threshold.**
- At d = 2.5 m on the site product, ρ = exp(−0.5 (2.5/250)²) ≈ 0.99995 and g_b ≈ −g_s ≈ cos²e/2.5 ≈ 0.4 rad/m.
- The second term, (1 − ρ²) g_s² σ_s², is at most about 0.004 σ_s rad. That is about 0.06° at the map's typical 0.26 m, and still only 0.34° at its 1.54 m maximum.
- The first term tends to g |σ_b − σ_s|. So 2.84° needs |σ_b − σ_s| ≈ 0.12 m between adjacent pixels of the total-error map, which is pixel-scale texture.
- The Phase 2 formula took σ_b and σ_s pointwise (nearest pixel). Then E[(δ_b − δ_s)²] → (σ_b − σ_s)² as d → 0: an error field that jumps at every pixel edge. That contradicts the approved assumption that errors are correlated over L_corr = 250 m (uncalibrated), which implies increments of about σ d/L, about 2.6 mm at 2.5 m.
- By Cauchy–Schwarz (|C| ≤ σ_b σ_s), no choice of correlation can restore continuity while the variances stay pointwise. The 0.15° threshold is valid under a self-consistent version of the approved model, which predicts about 0.06°.
- My own Phase 2 expectation ("σ_b ≈ σ_s for adjacent pixels") was wrong for this error map.

**Correction (assumptions unchanged).** Within the site product, the error field is δ(x) = σ̃(x) z(x).
- z is the approved unit-variance field with ρ(d) = exp(−d²/2L²).
- σ̃² = G_L * σ² is the error-map variance smoothed with a Gaussian of standard deviation L. It is NaN-aware (normalized convolution), truncated at 3L, and evaluated on cells of max(pixel, L/5).
- Properties:
  1. K(x, y) = σ̃(x) σ̃(y) ρ(x − y) is positive semi-definite.
  2. E[(δ_b − δ_s)²] = (σ̃_b − σ̃_s)² + 2 σ̃_b σ̃_s (1 − ρ) → 0 as d → 0, with both terms O(d/L), because |∇σ̃²| ≤ (max σ² − min σ²)/2 · √(2/π)/L.
  3. A constant map is unchanged, and the mean of σ² is preserved, so the uncertainty budget is not reduced.
- Blockers from other products keep their pointwise error-map value: ρ = 0 there, so pixel texture cannot create a spurious difference.
- Pointwise values are still reported (`sigma_block_m`, `site_sigma_m`) next to the model values (`sigma_block_model_m`, `site_sigma_model_m`).
- The regularization parameters (`VARIANCE_REGULARIZATION`) are in the horizon cache key and provenance.
- L_corr, the cross-product allowance, the 5° nonlinear limit, k = 1 and all horizon values are unchanged. The golden test still passes.

**Synthetic reproduction** (Site01 coordinates, 5 m DEM, error map of 0.26 m plus 0.07 m pixel texture, observer 0 m):

| Model | Max near-field sigma at 2.5 m |
|---|---|
| Pointwise | 2.011° (median 1.334°) |
| Regularized | 0.0615°, matching the prediction 0.4 × 0.268 × 0.01 rad |

Under the corrected model such a patch is averaged at the correlation scale, so it is no longer nonlinear. The scenes now use error regions much wider than L (60 m and 40 m), where the model itself predicts σ_e ≈ 8° and 7.7°.

### Cross-product statistics: method and region (Site01 5 m vs PGDA 90 80 m)

The local real-data diagnostic reported the cross-product allowance for PGDA 78 Site01 vs PGDA 90 80 m as n = 40,000, mean −0.0532 m, std 1.2227 m, RMS 1.2239 m. The 2026-11 audit had reported mean +1.0726 m, std 6.2428 m, RMS 6.3343 m (n = 23,572) and a full-footprint RMS of 6.9930 m. **These are different measurements, not one measurement that changed.** Nothing in the NASA data, the products or the allowance definition has changed.

| | 2026-11 audit (`test_5m_footprint_and_overlap_elevation_offsets`) | Allowance (`engine.horizon.cross_product_discrepancy`) |
|---|---|---|
| Lattice | Site01 5 m pixel centres, every 16 px (80 m) | same |
| Region | annulus 4–8 km from the footprint centre (−11000, −12000) | intersection of the two windows: the whole 16 × 16 km footprint |
| Points | 23,572 (also the full footprint for the 6.9930 m RMS) | 40,000 |
| 80 m value | **nearest pixel** (rasterio `sample`) | **bilinear** between 80 m pixel centres, any nodata neighbour excluded |
| Nodata / masks | NaN dropped; no point lost | NaN and implausible heights dropped; no point lost |
| Sign | 5 m − 80 m | fine − coarse (5 m − 80 m) |

**Why they differ.**
- *Nodata and masks: no effect.* The annulus holds exactly 23,572 lattice points and the full lattice exactly 40,000, so neither measurement lost any point to nodata or masking. This is confirmed by `test_real_grids_put_every_lattice_point_at_one_fixed_offset`.
- *Interpolation: the dominant effect on the spread.* The 5 m lattice points are at x = −18997.5 + 80j and y = −4002.5 − 80i; the 80 m centres are at x = −19000 + 80m and y = −4040 − 80m. So every lattice point lies at the same offset o = (+2.5, +37.5) m from the centre of the 80 m pixel that contains it. Nearest-pixel sampling therefore compares h(p) with h(p − o), which adds ∇h·o ≈ 37.5 m · ∂h/∂y to each difference. That is a slope term, not a disagreement between the products. The spread grows to about √(s_bilinear² + |o|² var(slope)).
  - With the measured numbers, the same full footprint gives 6.9930 m (nearest) against 1.2239 m (bilinear). That implies a slope term of √(6.993² − 1.224²) ≈ 6.89 m, i.e. a slope-component spread of about 0.18 (about 10°) along y. This is ordinary south-polar relief.
- *Region: affects the mean.* Under nearest-pixel sampling the mean shifts by o·mean(∇h) over the region, about 37.5 m × the region's mean y-slope. The 4–8 km annulus and the full footprint can also differ in their bilinear means. Separating these needs the real data.

**Which statistic is the approved one.** `CROSS_PRODUCT_SAMPLING` specifies bilinear interpolation (any nodata neighbour excluded) over the intersection of the site-product and blocker-product windows, with stride = coarse/fine pixels and at most 40,000 points. Only the new measurement matches this definition. The allowance for Site01 / PGDA 90 80 m is therefore **1.224 m** (mean −0.053 m, std 1.223 m). The audit's figures remain a correct description of what that test measures, nearest-pixel differences on the annulus, but they must not be quoted as the allowance. No value was changed to match either report.

**Consequences.**
- The allowance is comparable to the products' error maps (up to 1.54 m for the 5 m map and 1.09 m for the 80 m map). It is not "4–6 times" larger, as the Phase 2 planning text stated from the nearest-pixel figures.
- For a cross-product blocker 500 m away, the allowance contributes about 1.224/500 rad ≈ 0.14°, not the 0.73° quoted during planning.
- The README example and TRACEABILITY now quote the allowance and label the audit figures by method.

**Tests and tools.**
- `tests/test_phase2_cross_product_method.py` uses the real grid geometry. It checks:
  - the fixed offset (2.5, 37.5) m and the exact 40,000 / 23,572 point counts;
  - that the allowance is bilinear: zero on a tilted plane, where nearest-pixel sampling shows exactly ∇h·o = 7.625 m;
  - that on rough terrain nearest = bilinear + ∇h·o to first order, with a nearest-neighbour spread of about 37.5 m × std(∂h/∂y).
- `scripts/compare_cross_product_statistics.py` (read-only) prints all four combinations of region {window, annulus} × method {bilinear, nearest} on the locally registered data. It also prints the offset, slope statistics and the nearest-neighbour statistics predicted from bilinear + ∇h·o, and confirms that the production function equals "window / bilinear". Please run it on the real files to separate the region and method effects on the mean.

### Item 3: NaN-safe integration and interpolation

- **Power metrics (`engine/power.window_metrics`).**

### Item 1: Earth as an oblate spheroid (IERS 2010, frozen J2000 mean pole)

> **Superseded in Phase 4 (algorithm 1.5.0):** the frozen J2000 pole described here was replaced by the pole of date (IAU 2006 precession + IAU 2000B nutation) after the production-kernel check against IAU_EARTH failed; see *Phase 4: Earth pole of date*. The limb geometry, bounds and rate bounds below are unchanged.

- **Problem.** Earth was a circular disk of the equatorial radius, 6378.137 km. The polar radius is 21.4 km smaller, so seen from the Moon the disk is an ellipse whose semi-axes differ by up to 0.0032°. Near the pole Earth's elevation changes by only about 0.05°/h, so an edge error of that size moves Earth limb (DTE "any"/"full") events by minutes.
- **Model** (`engine/earth.py`, `settings.EARTH_*`).
  - **Radii:** IERS Conventions 2010 (TN 36, Table 1.1): a = 6378.1366 km, 1/f = 298.25642, b = 6356.7519 km.
  - **Pole:** labelled everywhere as the *frozen J2000 mean-pole approximation*: the ICRF/J2000 Z axis, with precession and nutation of the pole neglected (provenance `earth_pole`, `parameters.earth_model.pole_label`).
    - The ≈ 50.3″/yr figure is general precession *in longitude*. The pole itself moves about 20″/yr (θ_A ≈ 2004″ per century), plus nutation of order 10″.
    - The code uses no rate. The deviation ε(t) = ∠(J2000 Z, IAU_EARTH Z) is computed per epoch in `test_frozen_pole_against_iau_earth`, which needs the production `pck00011.tpc`. The demo kernel set has no Earth orientation, so this test is skipped here: **run it with the production kernels before merging.**
  - **Atmosphere:** not modelled (geometric line of sight to the solid Earth); recorded in provenance.
- **Limb geometry.**
  - In coordinates scaled by (1/a, 1/a, 1/b) the observer's tangent cone touches the unit sphere in a circle. Mapped back, the observer-to-limb vectors are exactly q(t) = Q0 + cos t QA + sin t QB.
  - The upper limb (maximum elevation) and lower limb (minimum elevation) are solved independently by a safeguarded solver: a 64-point bracket, Brent refinement, verification against the bracket samples, and a 16× dense fallback that is counted.
  - **Against SPICE `edlimb`** (200 epochs over one 18.6-year nodal cycle, four sites from 89.5° S to 20° S, dense 10⁵-point sampling plus golden-section refinement), each limb separately:
    - |Δρ_up| ≤ 5.0×10⁻¹⁶ rad and |Δρ_down| ≤ 4.4×10⁻¹⁶ rad (required: 1×10⁻⁹);
    - the full-limb deviation δ_max agrees within 1×10⁻¹² rad;
    - zero solver fallbacks.
- **Shape bounds.** β = asin(b/d) ≤ ρ_up, ρ_down ≤ α = asin(a/d) on every epoch. Synthetic poles give the exact closed forms:
  - pole along the line of sight: a circle of radius atan(a/√(d² − b²)), which is *below* α;
  - pole perpendicular in the vertical plane: atan(b/√(d² − a²));
  - pole perpendicular and horizontal: α.
- **Disk quantities: full ellipse.**
  - `earth_disk_kernel` gives the centre clearance c, and g_top / g_bot as the extremes of el − H(az) along the exact limb. The limb is cut at closed-form horizon-node crossings, and each piece is bracketed and refined with Brent.
  - The visible fraction f comes from azimuth strips: limb arcs with 20-point Gauss–Legendre in the limb parameter, horizon segments in closed form.
  - **Against an independent dense reference** (10⁶ limb points; 4×10⁵ azimuth strips): g to 1×10⁻¹² rad (measured ≈ 6×10⁻¹⁵) and f to 1×10⁻⁶ (measured ≈ 2×10⁻⁹).
- **Cap shortcut.** The cap has radius (ρ_up + ρ_down)/2, offset (ρ_up − ρ_down)/2. It is used only if δ_max ≤ 10⁻⁶°, E_g = δ_max(1 + S/c_min) ≤ 10⁻⁶° and E_f = (ΔA⁺ + ΔA⁻)/(A_C − ΔA⁻) < 5×10⁻⁶.
  - On real geometry δ_max is 2.3×10⁻³° to 2.8×10⁻³°, so the full ellipse is always used: 242 of 242 golden samples; no cap-path samples, hence no display-digit differences.
  - The cap path evaluates an exact circular cone with the same kernel.
  - **Property test** (10⁴ random limbs, eccentricity up to 0.2, flat, sloped, notched and two-notch horizons): |Δg| ≤ E_g and |Δf| ≤ E_f in every case. Maximum actual/bound: 0.85 for g and 0.12 for f.
  - **Correction to the plan.** The planar expansion 4δ/ρ is *not* a lower bound for E_f on the sphere: E_f = (4δ/ρ)(1 − ρ²/12 + …). The test checks the closed form against its spherical first-order term, δ sin ρ / sin²(ρ/2).
- **Frozen-pole bounds** (kernel-free property test, 10⁴ random geometries, pole rotations ε from 1″ to 1°, η = (α − β) sin ε):
  - **P1:** |Δρ_up|, |Δρ_down| ≤ η; maximum ratio 0.9998, so the bound is tight.
  - **P2:** |Δg| ≤ η(1 + S/c_min); maximum ratio 0.9997.
  - **P3:** |Δf| ≤ E_f,pole; maximum ratio 0.085.
  - **Correction to the plan:** δ_max moves by up to 1.006η, so "|Δδ_max| ≤ η" is false. The cap centre and ρ_cap also move by ≤ η, so the proved bound is 3η, and that is what is tested.
- **Event-rate bounds.** r0 for `earth_top` and `earth_bot` is the complete bound B = 1.5 × [ |ėl_c| + S|ȧz_c| + (1 + S/c_min)V_limb + S·α·√(1 − c_min²)/c_min²·(|ėl_c| + V_limb) + map term ].
  - V_limb = a|ḋ|/(d√(d² − a²)) + (α − β)(|ψ̇| + |λ̇|) + α|ψ̇|, where ψ is the position angle of the projected pole against the local vertical and λ the pole's tilt to the line of sight, with |λ̇| ≤ |ṙ̂| + |ṗ|.
  - **Two terms were completed during implementation** (both only enlarge B):
    - the 1/cos el term uses the limb point's own elevation rate, |ėl_c| + V_limb;
    - V_limb gained the tilt-change term (α − β)|λ̇|.
  - `rho_rate` is display-only for Earth. The eclipse functions use V_limb for Earth's radius rate.
  - **Tests** (`tests/test_phase3_earth_rates.py`):
    - centre rates and ψ̇ agree with Richardson differences to 10⁻³ relative;
    - per-limb radial speeds agree with differences of SPICE-edlimb ρ_up / ρ_down, and stay below V_limb;
    - V_limb bounds the motion of the limb's radial function about the centre;
    - **total:** B ≥ the one-sided 1 s differences of g_top and g_bot on both sides, at 10⁴ epochs × 2 limbs across 18.6 years, including two-notch horizons (maximiser jumps). Maximum |FD|/B = 0.67. The pre-Phase-3 single-radius r0 would have under-bounded in 1003 of these 20 000 checks;
    - poisoning Earth's `rho_rate` leaves every Earth r0 unchanged.
  - **Event timing:** Earth-set and Earth-rise windows of 3 days each, with flat, sloped and notched horizons (six runs). Every `earth_top` / `earth_bot` event is within 1 s of a brute-force scan (60 s grid, 1 s rescan near zero, 1 ms bisection), direction-aware and per limb. No missed or spurious events, and zero runtime estimate failures for Earth limb functions.
- **Golden (Phase 2 file unchanged; `test_analysis_outputs_unchanged_except_flags` now bounds the Earth fields).**
  - Identical: horizon, every Sun value, Earth az, el, distance and c, and Earth-centre events. Sun and Earth-centre events stay within the 1 s bisection tolerance.
  - **Earth radius:** Δρ_earth from −0.00281° to −0.00233°, inside [β − ρ_old, α_new − ρ_old].
  - **Earth visible fraction:** changes at 25 samples, at most 9.2×10⁻⁴, within E_f,pole between the old and new radii.
  - **Earth limb events:** same sequence. They moved by 153 s to 169 s, within the per-event bound (α_old − β)(1 + S/c_min)/|ġ| + 2 s, which is 182 s to 199 s.
  - **`earth_any` / `earth_full`:** interval edges and summaries stay within that bound. The summary field `hours` (a sum of bounded interval durations) was added to the bounded fields; it had been compared exactly by mistake.
  - The detector's 11 runtime estimate failures are all `ecl_contact`, unchanged from before (see V-14).

  1. *New code, fixed before commit:* the horizon-segment integral (cos H₁ − cos H₂)/k cancelled catastrophically when H₁ and H₂ differed only by rounding. It doubled one strip's horizon area (Δf = 0.057 in one property-test case). Replaced by sin(H̄)·ΔA·sinc(ΔH/2); regression test `test_horizon_segment_integral_is_cancellation_free`.

### Item 4: Sun–Earth joint visibility, and the solar disk fraction fix

- **Solar disk visible fraction (`visibility._disk_exact`): defect found in item 1, fixed.**
  - **Cause:** the closed-form antiderivative evaluated asin(q), q = cos E sin x / sin r, and separately atan2(cos r·v, √(sin²r − v²)). At the cap's azimuth extremes q → ±1, where asin is ill-conditioned: a rounding error of 10⁻¹⁶ in q becomes about 10⁻⁸ rad. The two terms rounded inconsistently, so the errors did not cancel.
  - **Effect:** a Sun-sized disk on a steep horizon had f = 0.4050631 instead of 0.4051626. On 3000 random cases, 4 were off by more than 10⁻⁶, the worst by 9.9×10⁻⁵.
  - **Fix:** the term is written G(θ) = θ − cos r·atan2(cos r sin θ, cos θ) with θ = asin(q). G is flat at θ = ±π/2, so an error in θ no longer reaches the area.
  - **Result:** the closed form now agrees with the independent exact-limb kernel (item 1) to better than 10⁻⁹ in f and 10⁻¹² rad in clearance on the same 3000 cases.
  - `disk_state` also returns f = 1 exactly when the whole disk is above every horizon node it spans. It already returned an exact f = 0 for the hidden case. The integral had given 1 − 3×10⁻¹².
  - **Golden window:** the Sun's visible-fraction series and every power series are identical; two power-indicator means change by 4×10⁻¹⁴ relative; event times move by at most 1 ms (bisection rounding).
- **Spherical lens (`spherical_cap_lens_area`).** This replaces the planar `circle_overlap_fraction`, which is kept only as the small-angle reference.
  - **Branches:** concentric (d ≤ 10⁻¹⁵), contained or internally tangent, disjoint or externally tangent, and the lens A = 2α₁(1 − cos r₁) + 2α₂(1 − cos r₂) − 2E.
  - **Stability:** every quantity is cancellation-free:
    - 1 − cos r = 2 sin²(r/2);
    - the angles come from atan2(√D, N), with the Gram determinant D = 4 sin s sin(s − r₁) sin(s − r₂) sin(s − d);
    - the spherical excess E from L'Huilier's formula;
    - no acos anywhere.
  - **Defect found while testing, fixed:** in the numerator N₁ = cos r₂ − cos d cos r₁, the term h_d ≈ d²/4 was added to h₁ before h₂ was subtracted, so it was lost for equal radii and tiny d. It is now grouped as 2h_d(1 − 2h₁) + 2 sin((r₁ − r₂)/2) sin((r₁ + r₂)/2).
  - **Results:**
    - against an independent 1-D spherical integral, relative error ≤ 9.3×10⁻¹⁰ (reference-limited) over radii 0.01° to 45° and every separation;
    - continuous within 10⁻¹²·cap across every branch boundary;
    - no NaN on 10⁶ random and degenerate cases;
    - exactly symmetric (the radii are ordered first);
    - non-increasing in d.
  - **Correction to the plan:** the planar formula matches to 10⁻⁶ only at 0.01°. At 1° caps the spherical correction is about 10⁻⁴, which is why it was replaced.
- **Earth as the occulter: exact ellipse instead of the planned single radius.**
  - The plan modelled Earth as a circle of its limb's radius toward the Sun's centre. Measured against a dense ray–spheroid test, that is off by up to 2.2×10⁻⁴ in the occulted share. Its rigorous sandwich (lenses at the smallest and largest limb radius over the solar disk) is up to 4.1×10⁻³ wide on the real 3 March 2026 eclipse. Both exceed the 10⁻⁵ tolerance.
  - The occulted share is therefore integrated against Earth's exact limb ellipse. At each azimuth Earth's two limb points come in closed form, so its elevation range is exact.
  - Exact branches:
    - no overlap if γ ≥ ρ_sun + r_max;
    - the Sun wholly behind Earth if γ ≤ r_min − ρ_sun;
    - the spherical lens when Earth is circular.
  - **Result:** occ agrees with an independent exact-ellipse reference to 1.5×10⁻¹⁰ (tolerance 10⁻⁹).
- **Visible solar fraction with terrain and Earth (`sun_joint_state`).**
  - Exact branches:
    - hidden by terrain or covered by Earth (0);
    - terrain clears the whole Sun (1 − occ);
    - Earth's overlap wholly above the terrain span (f_terrain − occ);
    - Earth's overlap wholly below it (f_terrain).
  - Only when terrain and Earth both cut the Sun is f integrated. The Sun's azimuth span is cut wherever the integrand changes form:
    - the Sun's and Earth's azimuth extremes, whose square-root ends are removed by x = m + h sin θ;
    - the crossings of the two limbs;
    - every horizon node;
    - every crossing of the horizon with either limb.
  - On each smooth piece, Gauss–Legendre in θ doubles from order 16 to 512 until two successive changes are below the tolerance (10⁻⁵). The method and error estimate are recorded in the parameters.
  - **Results:** against an independent 2–8×10⁶-strip reference, the three-way fraction agrees to about 10⁻¹⁰ (asserted ≤ 10⁻⁸), with error estimates around 10⁻¹³. Every branch is exercised on synthetic geometry and on the 3 March 2026 eclipse, seen from 80° S and from 60° E on the equator.
  - **Two integrator defects found while testing, fixed:**
    1. A midpoint rule with no cuts at the limb crossings converged irregularly, and twice in a row by chance. It reported 3×10⁻¹⁰ while the true error was 5×10⁻⁸. Fixed by the cuts plus Gauss–Legendre.
    2. A horizon crossing only 2.5×10⁻⁶ rad inside Earth's azimuth extreme was missed: the closed-form limb solver rounds to "no limb" exactly at the extreme. The exact extreme elevation is now used there.
  - Above 60° solar elevation the three-way case still uses the sampled cap, labelled "sampled (above 60 deg)". This never happens at south polar sites.
- **Eclipse events.**
  - `ecl_contact`, `ecl_centre` and `ecl_total` use Earth's limb radius toward the Sun's centre. That differs from first contact with the exact ellipse only at second order, about 10⁻⁶°, or 0.01 s.
  - Their rate bound gains (α − β)·|ψ̇|, with |ψ̇| ≤ relative speed / sin γ, for the radius turning with the Sun's position angle about Earth.
  - At the 3 March 2026 eclipse (V-14 eclipse test) the contacts move 4 s to 14 s from the mean-radius values. The brute-force reference uses the same radius.
  - **Combined clearance g_vis:** it now uses the exact radial distance outside the ellipse. Its one discontinuity, the switch from terrain clearance to the joint value, happens at the same contact as `ecl_contact`. With the switch at the ellipse's largest radius instead, it fell mid-step and caused 9 runtime estimate failures; at the shared contact there are 0.
- **Keys and provenance.** `joint_settings()` (method version, concentric threshold, both tolerances, Gauss–Legendre orders) is in every result ID. The parameters record the joint method counts and the largest adaptive error estimate.
- **Item 1 code:** unchanged except that the detector's Earth entry also stores the limb ellipse (in local coordinates) and α − β for the occultation. Additive; Earth disk values are unaffected.

### Item 2: limb-darkened power indicator (extension D01)

- **Problem.** `P_rel = f·(1 au/r)²·k` weights the visible solar area uniformly. The solar disk is limb-darkened, so while terrain or Earth hides part of the Sun, f misstates the share of flux received: a limb sliver is fainter than average, the centre brighter.
- **Coefficients (cited, not invented).**
  - **Source:** Neckel & Labs (1994), *Solar Physics* 153, 91–114, Table I (p. 98). The scan was read from NASA ADS: this session's network was given a personal "LUNA-OPS" environment allowing only the ADS hosts.
  - **Row used:** 541.760 nm, exactly as printed: A₀ … A₅ = 0.26073, 1.27428, −1.30352, 1.47085, −0.96618, 0.26384, with printed F/I = 0.8002. No interpolation. 541.760 nm is the tabulated wavelength nearest 550 nm (8.24 nm, against 9.95 nm for 559.950 nm).
  - **Verification:** all 30 rows were transcribed into a test fixture and checked against the paper's two printed relations, Eq. 5 (ΣA_n = 1) and Eq. 6 (F/I = 2ΣA_k/(k + 2) against the printed F/I column). 29 rows satisfy both. The selected row gives Σ = 1.00000 and F/I = 0.800153 → 0.8002.
  - **Misprint found in the source:** the 365.875 nm row violates both relations as printed (Σ = 0.99800). A₂ = 0.17682 instead of 0.17482 would satisfy both. It was confirmed at 300 dpi, kept as printed and is not used.
  - **Correction to the plan:** the paper tabulates no separate I(μ) values, so the planned "reproduce the paper's tabulated I(μ)" check is replaced by the two printed relations.
  - I(μ) is positive and increasing on [0, 1]. The limb-to-centre ratio is I(0)/I(1) = A₀ = 0.26073.
- **Normalisation.**
  - A point at angular distance s from the disk centre has sin θ = sin s / sin ρ exactly, so μ = √(1 − sin²s / sin²ρ).
  - Fluxes are integrated over solid angle, like f, and divided by the full-disk flux F = 2π sin²ρ ∫₀¹ I(μ) μ / √(1 − sin²ρ (1 − μ²)) dμ. Its small-disk limit F/(π sin²ρ) is the paper's F/I; at the Sun's size the spherical correction is 4.8×10⁻⁶ relative.
  - **Correction to the plan:** the plan wrote the planar form 2πρ²ΣA_k/(k + 2). The solid-angle form keeps f_I consistent with f, and with a uniform law it gives the cap area exactly (10⁻²⁰).
  - **Consequence:** f_I = 1 exactly for a fully visible Sun and 0 when hidden, so P_rel_LD = P_rel bitwise there.
- **Computation** (`engine/limb_darkening.py`).
  - f_I is a nested integral over the visible part of the disk:
    - outer, over azimuth, with the same cuts as item 4 (the Sun's and Earth's azimuth extremes, the limb crossings, horizon nodes and horizon–limb crossings);
    - inner, over each visible elevation segment, with exact ends (the Sun's cap and Earth's exact limb ellipse) and μ evaluated cancellation-free (haversines).
  - Both levels use the sine substitution, for the square root of μ at the limb, and Gauss–Legendre. The order doubles from 16 to 256 until two successive changes are below 10⁻¹⁰.
  - Above 60° solar elevation, intensity-weighted cap points are used (labelled; never at south polar sites).
  - Items 1 and 4 are unchanged: their helpers are reused read-only.
- **Results.**
  - Against an independent polar-coordinate reference (closed-form visible radial intervals for a level horizon and a circular Earth; Gauss–Legendre between the φ kinks), f_I agrees to 1.2×10⁻¹² at worst over slivers of 0.1 % to near-full disks, the centre chord (exactly ½) and Earth occultations with and without terrain.
  - With a uniform law, f_I reproduces `disk_state`'s f (to 10⁻¹⁰) and item 4's joint fraction (to 10⁻¹⁰).
  - **Limb sliver:** the flux/area ratio of a thin hidden limb sliver tends to A₀/(F/I) = 0.3259 with the analytic slope A₁·(3π/(8√2))/(F/I) = 1.327 per √depth. Measured: 1.298 at 0.05 % depth.
  - **Correction to the plan:** the planned uniform-midpoint polar reference converged too slowly where the horizon passes through the disk centre (1.4×10⁻⁸ off). It was made adaptive in φ; the engine agreed with itself at tolerances 10⁻¹⁰ and 10⁻¹³.
- **P_rel unchanged:** golden window, current code against the item 4 commit. The Sun's visible fraction, all power series and their unrounded metrics, the summary and every event are bitwise identical. P_rel_LD equals P_rel bitwise at all 240 fully visible or hidden samples. Over the month the limb-darkened equivalent full-Sun hours differ by at most 0.016 h. Largest adaptive error estimate: 1.2×10⁻¹³.
- **Outputs.**
  - `series.sun.f_ld`;
  - `series.power[model].series_ld` and `metrics_ld`;
  - `summary.power_ld`;
  - CSV columns `sun_visible_flux_fraction_ld` and `power_rel_ld_<model>`, with the label and citation in the CSV preamble;
  - `parameters.limb_darkening`: law, citation, wavelength, coefficients, normalisation, tolerance, label (*"Estimated (D01: Neckel & Labs 1994 Table I limb darkening at 541.760 nm, full-disk-flux normalised)"*), method counts and the largest error estimate.
  - `ld_settings()` is in every result ID.
  - Frontend types gain the optional fields.
- **Assumption (labelled):** one wavelength stands for the panel's spectral response. A broadband, cell-weighted law would need spectral response data.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_phase3_power.py` (16).

### Item 7: sub-bin azimuth peaks

- **Problem.** The horizon is computed at 3600 azimuth nodes (0.1°) and interpolated linearly between them. A peak narrower than a bin, standing between two nodes, is invisible to the disk model.
- **Definition.**
  - `SUBBIN_RAYS` = M, an integer from 1 to 16 (default 4), is the number of rays per bin interval [az_k, az_k+1), counting the node ray. The extra rays are at az_k + j·Δ/M for j = 1 … M − 1, so M = 4 samples every 0.025°.
  - M is validated at startup (environment `LUNAOPS_SUBBIN_RAYS`; 0, 17, 2.5, "4x" and booleans are rejected) and again when a horizon is computed. It is part of the horizon key and recorded in the horizon parameters.
- **Implementation** (`engine/horizon.py`).
  - The kernel gains an azimuth offset that defaults to 0.0. Node rays compute 2πk/n + 0.0, which is bitwise 2πk/n, so `h_deg`, D, W, incomplete, sigma and every Phase 2 array are bitwise identical for any M (tested for M = 1, 2, 4, 8 on a two-product scene with error maps).
  - Sub-ray j is the same kernel at offset 2πj/(Mn). Its elevations equal node 4k + j of a horizon with 4× the bins (to 10⁻⁹°) and an analytic brute-force horizon (to 0.01°).
  - Per bin, `subbin_excess_deg` = max_j (H_sub − [H_k + (j/M)(H_k+1 − H_k)])⁺. The last bin wraps to node 0. M = 1 gives exactly 0.
  - `subbin_unknown` is set when a sub-ray or either node ray is incomplete (insufficient terrain); the value is then only what those rays saw.
  - Both arrays are stored with the horizon (cache included) and exported in the horizon JSON. The parameters record the maximum excess, how many bins have an excess and how many are unknown.
- **Window flag** (`events.subbin_peak`), computed beside and never inside the Phase 2 flags. `interval_flags` is unchanged and still returns exactly its Phase 2 dictionary.
  - A window is flagged True if max_t (g(t) − E(t)) < 0. Here E is the largest excess over the bins the disk spans (its limiting edge) and g the window's clearance function: a peak between nodes as high as the one measured could hide the whole window.
  - The flag is None if an excess is unknown in a spanned bin, or was not computed. Gaps are always False, because a missed peak can only raise the horizon. Unknown intervals are None.
  - Overlap windows inherit it like `uncertain`. It is shown as `subbin_peak` on intervals (also in the CSV) and counted in `summary.*.subbin_peak_windows`.
- **Nested refinement.** For M → 2M every sub-ray azimuth is reproduced bitwise (doubling numerator and denominator is exact), so the excess is non-decreasing per bin, bitwise, over M = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Against an M = 64 reference the deficit is ≥ 0 and non-increasing.
  - A 1.9° spike centred mid-bin is caught exactly from M = 2.
  - **Correction to the plan:** the plan's "deficit ≤ 0.01° at M = 4 on the spike scene" holds only for a spike on the sub-ray grid. One between sub-rays (0.37 of a bin) still has a 0.56° deficit at M = 4, falling to 0 at M = 8. No fixed M can bound the deficit for features narrower than its spacing, which is why the excess is informational. The smooth crater satisfies the plan's 10⁻³° bound (M = 4 against M = 16).
- **Boundary cases (tested):**
  - a flat-topped horizon (a ridge wider than any bin) gives an excess below 10⁻³°;
  - a spike in the last bin is attributed to bin n − 1 (wrap to node 0);
  - a nodata strip seen only by a sub-ray makes that bin unknown while its nodes stay complete.
- **Golden window:**
  - events, interval states and edges, `uncertain`, `uncertain_reasons`, `near_field`, `brief` and the summary counts are identical for M = 1 and M = 4;
  - the largest excess on the smooth crater is 1.1×10⁻⁴°, with no unknown bins and no window flagged.
- **Cost:** an M = 4 horizon takes 2.5× the node-only time on the synthetic crater (best of three; acceptance ≤ 4.5×).
- **Tests:** `tests/test_phase3_subbin.py` (27).

### Item 6: DEM window loading (performance only; outputs bitwise unchanged)

- **Problem.** Every level was read as one fixed window: 80 m at ±250 km is about 156 MB, 240 m at ±600 km about 100 MB. The kernel then concatenated all windows into a second full copy, and the reader made float32 copies and full-size temporaries on top. Peak memory was about twice the terrain or more.
- **What stays fixed (the physics).**
  - Each product's nominal window: half-width `DEM_WINDOW_HALF_M` of its level, clipped to the product. It is not overridable.
  - The window list, its order and indices, every window's origin (x0, y0), pixel size, distance band (`band_m`) and therefore the step distances.
  - The stopping bound's h_max, still the maximum over the whole nominal windows, and the any-data test that decides the window list.
- **Changes** (`engine/dem.py`, `engine/horizon.py`).
  - **(a) One buffer.** Windows are read straight into slices of one preallocated float32 buffer, and the kernel uses it in place. Nodata, scale/offset and plausibility are applied in place in row chunks, with the same float32 operations element by element. No full-size temporaries remain: the any-data test and the maximum use `fmax`, which equals `nanmax` without its copy.
  - **(b) Initial reach and growth on demand.** A product listed in `DEM_INITIAL_REACH_M` is first loaded only within that reach (defaults 80 m: 100 km, 240 m: 200 km). Its nominal any-data flag and maximum come from a streaming pass over the whole nominal window, 512 rows at a time.
    - The loaded block keeps the nominal origin and an offset, and every sampler converts coordinates with the nominal origin. A pixel therefore has bitwise the same value and interpolation weights whether its block or the whole window is loaded.
    - In the kernel, a sample outside the nominal window is nodata, as before. A sample inside it but outside the loaded block stops that ray and names the window. The window's whole nominal block is then read, every window is repacked into a new shared buffer, and only those bins are recomputed. This is also how the item 7 sub-rays are handled.
    - The numpy readers (site height, `bilinear_many`, and the cross-product discrepancy, which reads the whole finer window and the coarser one over it) grow a partial block before reading.
  - **(c) Settings, performance only.** `LUNAOPS_WINDOW_MODE` is `adaptive` (default) or `full` (the reference: the pre-Phase-3 loader, kept verbatim). `DEM_INITIAL_REACH_M` is keyed by level or product_id, and must lie in (0, nominal half-width]: validated at startup for levels and at load for products.
    - Both are recorded in the horizon parameters as `performance_settings`, with each window's nominal and loaded pixel counts and every expansion.
    - Both are excluded from the horizon key, because the tests prove the outputs bitwise invariant. The nominal windows remain keyed.
  - **Diagnostic:** the kernel also returns `stop_step` (the step at which each ray stopped, broke or ended; `Horizon.stop_step`), with `stopping_diagnostics` in the parameters.
- **Verification** (`tests/test_phase3_windows.py`, 10 tests; every comparison is bitwise against the reference loader). Compared: H, D, W, HB, incomplete, `stop_step` (and its histogram), sigma and every Phase 2 array, the item 7 sub-bin arrays, h_max, the uncertainty statistics, ground height and the horizon key.
  - **Scenes:**
    - the synthetic crater, with initial reach 1, 10 and 50 km and nominal;
    - a 2 km summit observer whose rays run far past any small reach (expansions occur);
    - a coarse product with a nodata band and another product's edge exactly at the reach (and ±240 m around it);
    - `use_stop=False`;
    - a Phase 2 scene with error maps and a coarse reach (2 km) smaller than the fine window, which forces the cross-product reader to grow the block.
  - **Loader:** identical window keys, origins, bands, nominal shapes and step distances; bilinear values identical at 200 random points through a partial window. Invalid reaches (≤ 0, beyond nominal, NaN, strings) and window modes are rejected, and the horizon key ignores the performance settings but not the nominal windows.
- **Performance** (best of three, 3600 bins, tracemalloc peak during `compute_horizon` including loading):

  | Scene | Reference loader | Adaptive, default reach | Adaptive, small reach (1 expansion) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | synthetic crater (20 m ±45 km, 240 m ±300 km) | 1.35 s, 132 MB | 1.27 s, 65 MB (51 % lower) | 240 m at 50 km: 1.58 s, 110 MB |
  | 80 m ±90 km + 240 m ±240 km | 0.52 s, 73 MB | 0.42 s, 32 MB (57 % lower) | 40 / 50 km: 0.64 s, 29 MB |

  - The acceptance test asserts ≥ 40 % lower peak on the 80 m + 240 m scene at 360 bins: 42 % measured.
  - A reach small enough to force expansions saves memory only when rays do not need the far terrain, and costs time for the re-run bins and the streaming pass.
  - On real PGDA data (80 m nominal ±250 km), the saving from the default 100 km reach depends on how far the site's rays travel. Measure it with `scripts/check_window_identity.py`.

It reports time, peak memory and expansions, and exits 1 on any difference. On the synthetic crater it reports IDENTICAL for the default and a 50 km reach. It has not been run on the real PGDA files, which are not in this environment.

### Item 8: performance and code quality (scientific outputs unchanged)

- **Method: profile first.** cProfile of the 30-day golden analysis (synthetic crater, sandbox kernels): 15.1 s median of three runs with the horizon recomputed each time. 18.0 s of the 20.2 s profiled was the event detector's 8357 `evaluate` calls:
  - `earth_disk_state`, 5.6 s: the exact-ellipse kernel, run at every step with Earth's visible fraction requested;
  - `np.cross` on single 3-vectors, 4.5 s cumulative over 108 k calls, almost all of it argument and axis handling;
  - `enu_basis`, 1.7 s: the site's local basis recomputed at each of 16 718 `sample_target` calls;
  - `spkcpo` (SPICE), 1.4 s: essential.
  - `_smooth_1d` (the plan's suspect) does not appear. On a 5 m + 80 m scene with error maps, `regularized_error_sigma` takes 0.07 s of a 1.3 s horizon. It was left unchanged: no measured gain, and a vectorised replacement could not be guaranteed bitwise equal to `np.convolve`. `build_series` takes 0.2 s.
- **Optimisations, all bitwise output-preserving:**
  1. `frames.cross3`: the 3-vector cross product with np.cross's arithmetic in the same order (c₀ = a₁b₂ − a₂b₁, …), used in the per-step paths (`limb_ellipse`, `psi_rate`, `limb_radius_toward`). It is bitwise np.cross on 10⁶ random vectors, including broadcasting.
  2. `Site.basis` and `Site.pos_km` are computed once per site and handed out as copies. Callers cannot alter the cache, and a changed field recomputes. SpiceyPy refuses read-only arrays, so copies were chosen over read-only views.
  3. The detector requests Earth's disk state without the visible fraction: its event functions use only the clearances, which the kernel computes before and independently of the fraction. `full_state` (the rule states at interval points) recomputes the fraction from the same stored geometry, which gives the identical value.
  4. **Pruned Brent refinement in `earth_disk_kernel`.** All pieces are still sampled; a piece is refined only if its upper bound (best sample + L·h/2) can reach the current extreme.
     - L = ω(1 + S/c_min) bounds the clearance's rate along the limb:
       - ω = R/(|Q0| − R) bounds the limb direction's angular speed, with R = √(|QA|² + |QB|²);
       - S is the largest horizon slope over the span;
       - c_min = cos(|el(Q0)| + asin(R/|Q0|));
       - a 1 % margin covers rounding, and nothing is pruned near the zenith.
     - A pruned piece's maximum is provably below the final maximum, and `max` does not depend on order, so g_top and g_bot are bitwise the same.
     - Checked on 3000 random limbs and horizons (with and without f, circular cones included) and on a full 2-day analysis with pruning switched off. The kernel is 5.0× faster on 400 near-horizon cases.
  5. `HorizonProfile.abs_dh` keeps |H_k+1 − H_k| instead of rebuilding it at every `earth_disk_state` call.

### Earth pole of date (algorithm 1.5.0)

**Root cause of the A2 failure**
- Until 1.4.0, Earth's spin axis was the J2000 Z axis.
- The real pole precesses by about 20″ per year: 539″ from J2000 Z in 2026, 740″ in 2036, 1001″ in 2049 (against IAU_EARTH).
- A pole error ε moves limb points by up to η = (α − β) sin ε, where α − β ≈ 3.19×10⁻³° is Earth's angular flattening seen from the Moon. On a sloped horizon the clearance changes by up to η (1 + S/c_min).
- So the Phase 3 expectation of 1×10⁻⁵° was inconsistent with the model's own bound after about 2030. It had never run, because the production PCK was not installed in Phase 3.
- It was also 18 times the 1×10⁻⁶° tolerance applied to the Earth cap shortcut.

**Model**
- Earth's spin axis is now the Celestial Intermediate Pole (CIP) of date in the GCRS: the third row of the bias-precession-nutation matrix NPB = R1(−(ε_A + Δε)) R3(−(ψ̄ + Δψ)) R1(φ̄) R3(γ̄), multiplied out in closed form.
- This is exactly SOFA/ERFA `eraPn06(date, eraNut00b(date))`, implemented in `backend/lunaops/engine/earth_orientation.py` as cached numba kernels.
- **IAU 2006 precession:** Fukushima-Williams angles γ̄, φ̄, ψ̄ and the mean obliquity ε_A.
  - Sources: Capitaine, Wallace & Chapront 2003, A&A 412, 567; IERS Conventions (2010), IERS TN 36, eq. 5.40.
  - These are the ERFA `pfw06` and `obl06` polynomials. The frame bias at J2000.0 is included.
- **IAU 2000B nutation:**
  - Sources: McCarthy & Luzum 2003, Celest. Mech. Dyn. Astr. 85, 37; IERS Conventions (2010) §5.5.1; ERFA `nut00b`.
  - 77 luni-solar terms on the Simon et al. (1994) Delaunay arguments, plus fixed offsets of −0.135 mas and +0.388 mas in lieu of the planetary terms.
  - The table was extracted mechanically from the ERFA 2.0.1 source (SOFA release 2023-10-11, BSD licence), not retyped.
- **Time scale:** TT Julian centuries from J2000.0, from SPICE `unitim(et, "TDB", "TT")`.
- **Frames:**
  - SPICE `J2000` is taken as the ICRF/GCRS axes, as NAIF does for the DE ephemerides.
  - The J2000 → `MOON_ME_DE440_ME421` rotation and its derivative still come from SPICE `sxform`: p = R z and ṗ = Ṙ z + R ż.
  - ż, the CIP's own rate (at most about 1×10⁻¹¹ rad/s), comes from a ±3600 s central difference.
- **Determinism:** no Earth orientation is read from any kernel, so the demo and production sets give the same pole. A test spies on `sxform` to confirm that only J2000 → body-fixed is requested.
- **Provenance and keys:**
  - `EARTH_POLE_MODEL = "CIP-IAU2006-precession-IAU2000B-nutation"` is in the result key (it replaces `"frozen-J2000-mean-pole"`), and `earth_pole` and `parameters.earth_model.pole_label` carry the new label.
  - `ALGORITHM_VERSION` 1.5.0 is in both the horizon and result keys. Cached results from 1.4.0 are therefore not reused.
  - Horizon values do not depend on Earth, but the horizon key changes with the version.

**Accuracy and neglected effects**
- SOFA states that IAU 2000B gives the pole to 1 mas from 1900 to 2100.
- **Neglected:**
  - polar motion (about 0.3″) and free-core nutation (about 0.2 mas), which are unpredictable and not in any pole-of-date model;
  - the IAU 2006 adjustments to the nutation amplitudes (micro-arcseconds);
  - TT − TDB in the nutation argument, which is handled but would change the pole by under 10⁻¹⁴ rad;
  - Earth's atmosphere, as before.
- At Earth's angular flattening a 1 mas pole error moves the limb by about 1.5×10⁻¹¹°.

**Verification (`tests/test_phase4_earth_pole.py`, 8 tests; reference data in `tests/reference/`)**

  Shifts are larger on steeper horizons and in geometries where the governing limb point lies far from the projected axes.
- **Earth visible fraction:** changes by at most 2.2×10⁻⁶, below the 5×10⁻⁶ display precision.
- **Unchanged:** the Sun, the horizon and power. Eclipse contacts use Earth's limb radius toward the Sun, which moves by at most η (estimated ≤ 0.1 s; not measured).

**Cost**
- The pole is evaluated in about 40 µs per epoch (first version: 4.8 ms; vectorised: 0.13 ms; numba kernels: 0.04 ms).
- `pole_in_frame` takes about 72 µs per single epoch, including the SPICE transform.
- One Earth sample (`sample_target`) costs about 75 µs more at one epoch per call (430 against 350 µs) and about 15 µs more per epoch in batches of 400.
- A 30-day event detection (site −89°, 45°, sloped horizon, November 2026) takes 1.8% longer: the median of three alternating runs in one process was 11.64 s against 11.43 s.

**Limitations**
- **Remaining pole error:** IAU 2000B's 1 mas, plus polar motion of about 0.3″ (ITRF pole vs CIP). At Earth's flattening that is at most about 5×10⁻⁹° of limb, negligible.
- **IAU_EARTH as reference:** the production-kernel check can only confirm the pole to the IAU_EARTH model's own accuracy (precession only, about 10″). The model's accuracy rests on the ERFA comparison.
